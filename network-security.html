Core Topic

Network Security

Network security is about protecting the systems and data that travel across your network. Whether you are learning to defend a home lab or preparing for a security role, understanding how traffic moves — and where it can be intercepted or blocked — is essential.

This guide covers the foundational ideas every beginner should know before diving into tools like Nmap or Wireshark. Always practice on networks you own or have explicit permission to test.

Key Concepts

  • OSI & TCP/IP models — Layers that describe how data is packaged, routed, and delivered across networks.
  • IP addressing & subnets — How devices are identified and grouped on a local or wide-area network.
  • Ports & services — Entry points where applications listen for connections (e.g., port 443 for HTTPS).
  • Firewalls & ACLs — Rules that allow or deny traffic based on source, destination, and protocol.
  • Network scanning — Discovering live hosts and open ports to map the attack surface (use only on authorized targets).
  • Packet analysis — Inspecting raw traffic to diagnose issues, detect anomalies, or understand protocols.
  • IDS/IPS basics — Systems that detect or block suspicious activity on the network.
  • VPNs & encryption in transit — Protecting data as it moves between endpoints over untrusted links.
