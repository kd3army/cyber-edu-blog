Core Topic

Cryptography

Cryptography is the science of protecting information through mathematical techniques. It underpins nearly every secure system — from password storage and HTTPS to digital signatures and encrypted messaging.

As a beginner, focus on understanding when and why to use different cryptographic tools rather than implementing algorithms from scratch. Misusing crypto is one of the most common sources of real-world vulnerabilities.

Key Concepts

  • Symmetric encryption — Same key encrypts and decrypts (e.g., AES). Fast, but key distribution is a challenge.
  • Asymmetric encryption — Public/private key pairs (e.g., RSA, ECC). Used for key exchange and digital signatures.
  • Hashing — One-way functions that produce fixed-size digests (e.g., SHA-256). Used for integrity checks, not encryption.
  • Password hashing — Slow, salted algorithms like bcrypt or Argon2 to resist brute-force attacks on stored credentials.
  • Digital certificates & PKI — How browsers trust HTTPS sites through certificate authorities and chain of trust.
  • TLS/SSL — Protocol that encrypts data in transit between clients and servers.
  • Encoding vs. encryption — Base64 and hex are not security; they do not protect data from being read.
  • Common pitfalls — Rolling your own crypto, weak keys, ECB mode, missing salt, and deprecated algorithms (MD5, SHA-1 for security).
